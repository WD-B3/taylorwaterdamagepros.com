How to Find a Hidden Water Leak in Your House: A Taylor, MI Guide

Most hidden leaks give themselves away with a bill, a meter or a smell. Here is how to check each one.

By the Taylor Water Damage Pros team

☎ (734) 865-2522

A hidden water leak is one you cannot see: inside a wall, under a floor, behind an appliance or in a line under the slab. They are costly because they run for weeks before anyone notices. The good news is that most can be found with a handful of simple checks that need no tools.

Start with the water meter

The most reliable test is the meter. Turn off every water-using fixture and appliance in the house, including the ice maker and the dishwasher.

Find the meter, usually in a basement, utility room or an outdoor pit, and read the dial or the small leak indicator. If it moves with everything off, water is going somewhere. Check again after an hour without using water. A moving meter means a leak between the meter and the fixtures.

Ask your water utility how to find and read your meter if you are not sure. A utility can also tell you whether a jump in your water bill matches your usage, which is a second clue.

A toilet that runs on its own or hisses after flushing is the most common household leak, and it costs nothing to test. Appliance lines are the second most common. The appliance leak page explains what to do when one fails and how it spreads.

Signs inside the house

  • A musty smell with no visible source.
  • Warm spots on the floor, which can point to a hot water line under the slab.
  • Paint that blisters or wallpaper that peels.
  • Baseboards or flooring that swell or cup.
  • The sound of water running when nothing is on.
  • A water bill that rises with no change in use.

A warm floor spot or a meter that runs with the house shut off deserves a plumber who does leak detection. The slab leak page explains what happens when the leak is under the concrete. A damp wall with no obvious source belongs with a moisture reading, as explained on the wall and drywall page.

When tools help

Pros use two tools to map a hidden leak. A moisture meter reads how wet drywall and wood are, and a thermal camera shows cool areas where water is evaporating. A plumber's listening device and pressure test find leaks in lines.

None of these replace a plumber for the repair, but they turn a guess into a location. Restoration crews use the first two to map the damage, and plumbers use the last two to find the source.

After you find it

  1. Shut off the water to the leaking fixture, or at the main if you cannot find the shutoff.
  2. Photograph the source and the damage before anything is moved.
  3. Call a plumber for the repair.
  4. Call a restoration crew to dry the wet materials, which are a separate job.
  5. Tell your insurer what you found and when, and ask what applies.

A simple weekly habit

Once a month, look under every sink with a flashlight, touch the base of each toilet and glance at the ceiling below each bathroom. Check the water heater base and the washer hoses at the same time. The whole round takes ten minutes and catches most slow leaks while they are still small and cheap.

Add the meter check twice a year, and any time a bill jumps. Write the reading and the date on a card near the meter. A trend that creeps up with no change in how you live is the signal that something is leaking, even if the house looks perfectly dry.

Questions people ask

Where is my water meter in a Taylor, MI house?

It is commonly in a basement or utility room near where the line enters, or in an outdoor pit. The City of Taylor water department can tell you how to find and read yours.

How does a toilet dye test work?

Put a few drops of food coloring or a dye tablet in the tank, wait fifteen minutes without flushing and check the bowl. Color means the flapper is leaking.

Is a high water bill always a leak?

No. Guests, seasonal use and irrigation raise bills. A meter that moves with everything off points to a leak.

Who finds the leak and who fixes the damage?

A plumber finds and repairs the leak. A restoration crew dries and repairs the damage it caused.
