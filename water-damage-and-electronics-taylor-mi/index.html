What Water Damage Does to Electronics and When to Unplug: Taylor, MI

Water and power do not mix, and a wet device can look fine while it fails. Here is what to do, in order.

By the Taylor Water Damage Pros team

☎ (734) 865-2522

Electronics after water damage fail in two ways. Water causes a short circuit that can destroy the device and create a shock or fire hazard, and moisture left inside causes corrosion that shows up weeks later. Both are reasons to unplug first, and not to power on a wet device to see if it works.

Safety first: the panel and the outlets

If water is touching an outlet, a cord or the electrical panel, do not touch anything. Do not stand in water to reach a breaker. Call the electric utility or 911, and ask an electrician to check the system before power is restored to the wet area. Our crews are not electricians, and they will not turn power back on in a wet area until an electrician has cleared it.

  • Turn off power at the panel only if it is dry and you can reach it without standing in water.
  • Unplug devices only when the outlet and cord are dry and the floor is dry.
  • If a plug is underwater, leave it and call for help.
  • Never use a wet appliance, a wet extension cord or a wet power strip.
  • Have an electrician inspect any outlet, switch or panel that got wet.

What to do with the devices

DeviceWhat to doWhat to avoid
Phone or tabletPower off, remove the case, dry the exteriorRice, heat and charging it
LaptopPower off, unplug, remove the battery if possibleTurning it on to test
TV and monitorsUnplug and leave for a technicianPlugging in after drying the surface
AppliancesUnplug and have a technician evaluateRunning them to see if they work
Hard drives and storagePower off and take them to a data recovery professionalOpening them or using a hair dryer

Heat is the usual mistake. A hair dryer or an oven can warp components and push water deeper into a device. Silica gel packets help absorb moisture around a device in a sealed container, but they do not fix a wet one. For anything that matters, a technician is the right next step, and the earlier the better.

Why a working device can still be dangerous

A device that powers on after getting wet is not necessarily safe. Corrosion starts quickly, and a unit with water inside can short later, overheat or shock someone. Appliances with heating elements, motors and controls are the most concerning. Plan to have such items checked before use, even if they appear fine.

For losses that started with a pipe or a heater, the water heater page covers the electrical shutoff steps. If wet electronics are part of an insurance claim, keep them, photograph them and list them before discarding, as the next section explains.

What technicians can sometimes do

A repair shop can open a device, clean the board with solvent and dry it under controlled conditions. The sooner it is brought in, the better the chance. Devices left powered while wet, or left sitting in a damp room for days, are much harder to save.

Back up what you can before anything else. If a phone or laptop still works, copy photos and files to another device straight away, then power it down and take it in. If it does not turn on, tell the shop what happened, how long it was wet and whether it was ever charged.

Smoke and carbon monoxide alarms

Wet alarms and battery backups can fail. After a loss, test each alarm and replace any that were soaked. Keep them working while the house dries, because a wet home with portable heaters and extension cords adds risks that a working alarm helps cover.

Keep a list of serial numbers and model names in a place that is not on the device. If a loss happens, that list saves time with the insurer and the repair shop. Photos of your setup before a loss, such as the TV wall or the home office, also help prove what was there.

Taylor, MI: keep the evidence and ask your insurer

Michigan's insurance regulator advises photographing damaged items and keeping them until your insurer says you can discard them, and the advice applies to electronics. Make a list with the brand, model, age and approximate value. Contents coverage often has limits, and a backup endorsement may have its own limit, so ask your agent how electronics are treated.

If you rent in Taylor, your renter's policy, not the landlord's, usually covers your own devices. Tell the landlord about the water, and check your own policy. Sources used: Michigan DIFS guidance and U.S. Census Bureau data showing a third of Taylor homes are renter-occupied.

Prevention

Keep electronics off basement floors, use shelves and raised surfaces, and put surge protection on outlets above the floor, not on it. A sump pump with a battery backup and a high-water alarm protect the whole lower level. Crews that work in Garden City and Inkster see the same pattern, which is that stored electronics at floor level are the first to go.

Questions people ask

Should I put a wet phone in rice?

It is not a reliable fix. Power it off, remove the case, dry the outside and take it to a technician.

Is it safe to turn the power back on after a flood?

Not until an electrician has inspected the system. Ask the utility and an electrician before restoring power to a wet area.

Can wet electronics be covered by insurance in Taylor?

They may be, as part of contents coverage. Keep the items, photograph them and ask your agent how your policy treats electronics.

How soon should a wet laptop be checked?

As soon as possible. Corrosion starts quickly, and a technician may be able to clean the board if the device stays off.
