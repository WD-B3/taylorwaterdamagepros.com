Garden City homes built in the boom years

Garden City is a western suburb about 15 miles west of downtown Detroit, and it grew faster in the 1950s than almost anywhere in Wayne County. The Census counted 9,012 people in 1950 and 38,017 in 1960.

That jump suggests a large share of the city's housing dates from that decade, which matters for water damage because a house that old often still has original supply lines, a replaced-once water heater and drywall that was hung over plaster or lath.

The 2020 Census counted 11,669 housing units in 5.86 square miles, with 3.7 percent of them vacant. That is a dense, mostly occupied city, so a leak in one house tends to be noticed quickly, but a slow leak in a tight lot can still spread through a shared fence line or a finished basement. Water damage restoration in Garden City starts with the age of the pipes and the way the house is built.

When water is spreading in Garden City

A pipe that lets go in an older Garden City house is the usual start. Galvanized and older copper lines corrode from the inside, and joints weaken, so the failure is often at a fitting or elbow. If you hear running water or see a stain growing on a ceiling, shut off the main and call. The burst pipe page walks through the shutoff, and the cleanup follows the plan on the water extraction page.

Basements are the other regular call. Many mid-century houses have unfinished or partly finished basements with floor drains, sump pits and water heaters at the bottom of the house, so one failure can put several things at risk. The basement water damage page covers pumps, finished rooms and contents, and the sewage backup page covers dark water from drains.

Before we arrive: if water is still spreading

Stop the source

Close the main water valve or the valve at the fixture, and shut the water heater off at its breaker or gas control.

Power safety

Turn off power to wet areas only if the panel is dry and reachable. If water is near it, stay out and call the utility or 911.

Lift and move

Raise valuables, papers and electronics off wet floors, and take photos before moving anything.

Keep clear

Keep people and pets out of dirty or standing water, then call the 24/7 line.

Landmarks and what they tell you about local water risks

Garden City's history is tied to Ford and Middlebelt Roads, where the Folker Building was the first city hall, and to Cherry Hill Road, where the first Little Caesars opened in 1959.

The corner of Ford and Middlebelt is also a commercial intersection with older storefronts. Older commercial buildings along those arterials share the same issues as older houses, such as aging roofs and drain lines, and the commercial page applies to any store whose stockroom is getting wet.

The city was a village in Nankin Township from 1927 and became a city in 1933, and Henry Ford's honeymoon cottage was moved there from Dearborn in 1952. These are facts about the place, not water facts. The point for a homeowner is that this is an old, established suburb, and the right assumption is that the plumbing and roofs are older than they look.

Cost factors in Garden City

Price follows the water category, the area affected, the materials, the drying days and the rebuild. An older house adds one factor, which is that plaster, old paint and original flooring can complicate demolition and matching. Scope and price are explained before work starts.

Ask your insurer what applies to your loss, and take photos before anything is moved. The state regulator advises telling your insurer you have water damage rather than calling it a flood, and keeping damaged items until the insurer says otherwise. Questions people ask

Do you cover every ZIP code in Garden City?

Yes. The covered ZIP codes are 48135 and 48136, from the Ford Road and Middlebelt Road area through the residential streets around Cherry Hill Road. 48136 is a PO box ZIP, so street addresses fall in 48135.

Is emergency water removal available in Garden City?

Yes. The dispatch line is open 24 hours a day, every day, and the dispatcher can send a crew to Garden City from Taylor when one is available. Call and describe what you see.

Are older Garden City houses more likely to have plumbing leaks?

Older supply lines can corrode and fail at joints, so age is a factor. It is not a guarantee, and a plumber can tell you what is in your house.

Does my water heater leak count as sudden damage?

Often it can, when the tank fails without warning. Insurers differ, so ask yours. Slow leaks are often excluded.
