Water Damage Restoration Near Heritage Park, Taylor, MI

Burst pipes, appliance leaks, backups and heavy-rain intrusion around the Pardee Road side of Taylor.

☎ (734) 865-2522

Heritage Park is Taylor's best-known green space, at 12111 Pardee Road, between Goddard Road and Northline Road. People use the name as a landmark when they give directions, so this page uses it the same way. It covers homes and small buildings in the Pardee Road area of Taylor, described by landmarks and not by official boundaries, since no source we found defines one.

Where this area is

The park has its southern entrance on Northline Road. It holds Coan Lake, an artificial lake named for the first landowner in the area, along with a near replica of the old Hand train station and the 1882 West Mound church, which was moved there in 1994. The Junior League World Series is played at the park each August.

Those landmarks help when you call. If you tell dispatch you are near Heritage Park, give the cross street too, because the park is large and the surrounding streets are many. We will confirm the exact address before a crew is sent.

What the public data says about the homes nearby

NeighborhoodScout publishes figures for an area it calls Heritage Park, which it defines as a census tract. Its tract boundaries may not match how locals use the name. For that tract it reports mostly single-family homes and townhomes, many built between 1940 and 1969, and some built before 1940. It reports that most homes are owner-occupied and that vacancy runs around 10 percent.

Citywide, one housing guide gives a median build year of 1963 for Taylor, with three-bedroom ranches common. These are descriptions of housing stock, not predictions of damage. A newer house can flood from a failed supply line, and an older one can stay dry for decades. Basement prevalence for this specific area is not verified, so we do not assume it.

Storm history that is documented

We have no record naming Heritage Park or its streets in a flood. What is documented is city-wide. On August 11, 2014, heavy rain brought more than 4 inches to Taylor in a day, and the city reported nearly 200 residents contacting it about damage. On May 1, 2019, a local news report described 5 to 6 inches of water in some Taylor basements after heavy rain, without naming streets.

The lesson for a homeowner near Pardee Road is practical and not alarming. Heavy rain can overwhelm drains anywhere in the region, so know where your sump or floor drain is, and call quickly if water enters. Neighborhood-specific FEMA flood-zone status not verified. You can look up any address on FEMA's Flood Map Service Center, and a Zone X result is not a guarantee that water cannot enter.

The problems we are most often called about

  • A burst or frozen supply line that floods a floor in minutes.
  • A water heater tank or fitting that leaks at the base.
  • A washer hose, dishwasher line or refrigerator line that fails.
  • Basement water after heavy rain or a sump pump stoppage.
  • A sewer backup, which needs protective gear and is covered on the sewage backup page.

This list is general. It is not a claim that these failures are more common here than elsewhere in Taylor. The first steps are the same either way, and they are laid out in the next section.

First steps while you wait

  1. If water touches an outlet, cord or panel, stay out and call the utility or 911.
  2. Shut off the main water valve only if you can reach it safely and dry.
  3. Keep people and pets away from standing water, and treat sewage or storm water as contaminated.
  4. Photograph the damage from a safe spot before anything is moved.
  5. Lift small valuables off wet floors only where it is safe to do so.
  6. Call and describe the source, the rooms and the street.

Do not use a household vacuum on wet carpet, and do not rely on fans for sewage or flood water. Those jobs need trained crews and protective equipment. The flood damage page explains how contaminated water is handled.

How extraction and drying work

Crews remove standing water first, using pumps and wet vacuums, then take moisture readings in the floor, walls and framing. Air movers and dehumidifiers follow, and readings continue each day until the materials reach a normal level. Drywall can be dried in place only if it is not swollen and the seams are intact, otherwise it is cut out. The structural drying page has the detail.

Insurance records

Photos, moisture readings and a written scope can help describe the loss to your insurer. Whether the loss is covered depends on the cause and on your policy, and only your insurer decides. Michigan's insurance regulator notes that flood is not covered by a standard homeowners policy and advises telling your insurer you have water damage. Ask your agent how a sewer backup or sump failure is treated.

If the loss involves a city sewer system, a written notice may be due within 45 days under Michigan law, as the sewage backup page explains. For pipe failures, see the burst pipe page.

Questions people ask

Is the Heritage Park area in a FEMA flood zone?

Neighborhood-specific FEMA flood-zone status not verified. FEMA's Flood Map Service Center shows the mapped zone for a single address, and a Zone X result does not mean flooding cannot happen.

Do you cover the streets around Heritage Park and Pardee Road?

Yes, this is part of Taylor and the same crews cover it. Give dispatch the street address and the nearest cross street so the location can be confirmed.

Has the Heritage Park area flooded before?

We found no source naming it. Documented heavy rain in Taylor in August 2014 and May 2019 affected homes across the city, and the reports do not name streets here.

What should I do first if my basement takes on water after a storm?

Stay out of the water if outlets or a panel may be wet, keep pets away and photograph from a safe spot. Then call so a crew can extract the water and start drying.

Does my insurance cover a sump pump failure?

It depends on your policy. Michigan's regulator says a sewer backup or failing sump pump may be covered, and that a backup endorsement often has limits. Ask your agent before a claim.
