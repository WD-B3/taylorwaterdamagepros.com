Water Damage Restoration

Water damage restoration in Taylor, MI, from the first call to the finished repair

Water damage restoration is the work of removing water from a building, drying the materials it soaked and repairing what cannot be saved.

For a home or business in Taylor, MI, that usually means extraction pumps and wet vacuums on day one, then air movers and dehumidifiers for several days, and finally the drywall, flooring and paint that bring the room back. The industry standard for this work is ANSI/IICRC S500, and you can ask any company you call whether its crews follow it.

Taylor Water Damage Pros runs a 24/7 dispatch line for the city and the Downriver towns around it.

Most calls fall into a few patterns: a supply line that split behind a wall, a water heater that gave out, a basement that took on water during a hard rain, a sewer line that backed up, or a roof leak that finally showed itself as a stain on the ceiling. Each one has its own safety steps and its own drying plan, so each has its own page on this site.

If you already know your problem, start with the closest match. A line that is still spraying belongs on the burst pipe page, standing water in a room belongs on water extraction, and a damp wall with no obvious source is covered under wall and drywall repair. If you are not sure, call and describe what you see.

The first 24 to 72 hours after water gets in

The first day decides how large the job becomes. Water that is removed quickly stays in the flooring and the lower few inches of the walls. Water that sits spreads upward through drywall by wicking, soaks into subfloor seams and starts to feed mold.

EPA guidance on responding to water damage works from a 24 to 48 hour window for drying wet materials before mold gets a start, and it treats that window as a guideline rather than a guarantee. That is why dispatch runs around the clock.

  1. Hour 0 to 2: stop the source, shut off the main valve if needed, make the area safe and call the 24/7 line.
  2. Hours 2 to 12: crews arrive, check for electrical and contamination risks, and extract standing water with pumps, wet vacuums or truck-mounted units.
  3. Day 1 to 2: wet carpet pad, saturated insulation and ruined drywall are removed, and moisture meters and thermal cameras map what is still wet.
  4. Day 2 to 5: air movers and LGR dehumidifiers run while the crew checks readings daily, a process described on the structural drying page.
  5. After drying: documented readings confirm the structure is dry, and the repair work starts, from drywall and paint to flooring.

Every step is written down, with photos and moisture readings, because that record is also what your insurance adjuster will ask for. Nothing here replaces your own safety judgment. If water is touching outlets or an electrical panel, stay out, and call the utility or 911 before you call us.

Where water damage starts in Taylor homes

The source of the water decides the plan. Pressurized lines fail differently from drains, and a slow leak under a sink behaves nothing like a storm surge. Dispatchers sort calls by source first, then by what the water touched.

  • Pipes and plumbing: a burst pipe, a frozen line that split during a Michigan cold snap, a water heater leak or a slab leak under the concrete.
  • Appliances and HVAC: failed washer hoses and ice maker lines on the appliance leak page, clogged condensate drains on the AC leak page and soaked cabinets on the kitchen page.
  • Weather and building envelope: flooding, storm-driven rain, a ceiling stain from a roof leak, a wet basement or a damp crawl space.
  • Contaminated water: sewage backup is Category 3 water and needs protective gear and disinfection, not a shop vacuum.
  • Businesses: stores, offices and multi-unit buildings follow the plan on the commercial water damage page.

Know Before You Call

What drives the cost of water damage restoration

Nobody can quote an honest number for water damage without seeing it, and anyone who does is guessing. What can be explained up front is what moves the price. These five factors account for most of the difference between a small job and a large one.

FactorWhat it changes
Water categoryClean supply water, grey water and sewage need different protection, disposal and cleaning. Category 3 costs more to handle.
Area affectedMore wet square footage means more extraction time, more drying equipment and more materials to replace.
Materials involvedCarpet and drywall are quick to remove and replace. Hardwood, plaster and subfloors take longer to dry or save.
Drying daysEquipment is placed for the number of days the readings require, so a longer dry-out adds equipment time.
Rebuild scopeMitigation stops the damage. New drywall, flooring, trim and paint are a separate part of the scope.

To get your own number, call the dispatch line and describe the loss. The scope and price are explained before work starts, and the written scope is what you can hand to your insurer. Cost ranges quoted on random websites are averages across the whole country and do not reflect your house.

Insurance Claims

Water damage and your insurance claim

Documentation your adjuster can use

We photograph the damage, record moisture readings and write a scope of work. You stay in direct contact with your insurer and decide how to proceed with your claim.

Michigan's insurance regulator, the Department of Insurance and Financial Services, says flood damage is not covered under a standard homeowners policy and that water or sewer backup endorsements offer limited coverage. A sewer backup or a failing sump pump may be covered under your policy, which is why DIFS advises against labeling all water damage a flood.

Its practical advice is simple. Call your insurer or agent with your policy number ready, tell them you have water damage and want to know what coverage applies, protect the property from further loss, take photos or video, and do not discard damaged items until the insurer says you can. Your own policy language is the final word on what applies to your loss.

Common Questions

Water damage questions from Taylor homeowners

Do you handle water damage emergencies at night or on weekends in Taylor?

Yes. The dispatch line at (734) 865-2522 is answered 24 hours a day, every day of the year, including holidays. Water does not wait for business hours, so a burst pipe at 2 a.m. gets the same attention as a leak on a Tuesday afternoon. Same-day response is offered when crews are available, and the dispatcher will tell you what to do while you wait.

What should I do first when I find water in my house?

Stop the source if you can, starting with the main water shutoff. Then turn off power to the wet area only if you can reach the panel without standing in water. Lift small items off wet floors, keep children and pets out, and call the dispatch line. Photos and short videos of the damage taken before anything is moved will help later with your insurer.

How much does water damage restoration cost in Taylor?

It depends on the water category, the square footage affected, the materials involved, how many days of drying are needed and how much rebuilding follows. A small clean-water leak caught the same day is a very different job from a basement that sat wet for a weekend. Call the dispatch line and describe the loss, and the scope and price are explained before work starts.

Will my homeowners insurance pay for water damage?

Often, when the water was sudden and accidental, such as a burst pipe or a failed water heater. Gradual leaks and neglect are often excluded, and flood damage is usually excluded unless you carry separate flood coverage. Michigan regulators also point out that sewer or water backup coverage is usually a limited endorsement. Check your policy and ask your insurer what applies to your loss.

How long does it take to dry a home after water damage?

Drying a structure commonly takes about three to five days when the work starts early, and longer for dense materials such as hardwood, plaster and thick framing. The crew checks moisture readings each day and moves or removes equipment only when the readings reach the dry standard for your home, not on a fixed schedule.

Is it a flood or just water damage, and does the word matter?

It can matter a great deal for coverage. Michigan's insurance regulator advises telling your insurer you have water damage and asking what applies, rather than labeling the event a flood. For the cleanup itself, rising water from outside and a failed pipe inside are handled differently because outside water is usually contaminated.

Can I dry the carpet myself instead of calling a restoration company?

For a small, clean-water spill that is cleaned up within hours, a wet vacuum and a few fans may be enough. Anything that has soaked into the pad, wicked up the drywall or involved a dirty source needs moisture meters and proper equipment, because wet materials that look dry on the surface can stay damp inside the wall for weeks.
