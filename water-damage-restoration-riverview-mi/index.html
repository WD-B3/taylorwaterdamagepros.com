Where Riverview sits and why it matters for water

Riverview is a small city of about 12,490 people on 4.67 square miles, roughly 14 miles southwest of downtown Detroit, with West Jefferson Avenue running along its easternmost edge beside the Detroit River. Fort Street, which locals know as M-85, runs south to north through the middle of the city, and I-75 is reached at exit 34B on Sibley Road. That puts the city on the Downriver shoreline and south of Taylor.

Being near a river is the main local fact for water damage. Properties close to the water may sit in a mapped flood zone, and the FEMA Flood Map Service Center shows the zone for each address. The city's building department can answer questions about rebuilding rules. FEMA adds that you do not have to be in a mapped zone to flood, so a lot is only part of the story.

When water is spreading in Riverview

A river city has a few distinct emergencies. Heavy rain can overload drains and push water into basements, a storm can drive water through the building envelope and a frozen line can crack in the colder months. The flood damage page describes the contaminated-water side, and the storm water page covers wind-driven rain that gets in through the roof and walls.

Call, then act. Shut off the main if a pipe is involved, keep power off in wet areas where it is safe to do so and move valuables up. After-hours calls are routine. Same-day response is offered when crews are available, and the line is open all night.

Before we arrive

Find the valve

Locate the main water shutoff and close it if a supply line is the source.

Mind the panel

If water has reached outlets or the panel, do not touch them. Call the utility or 911 first.

Photograph first

Take photos and short videos before moving belongings, then lift what you can off wet floors.

Avoid dirty water

River-side flooding is often contaminated. Keep out of it and wait for protective gear.

A short history that explains the neighborhood

Riverview was incorporated as a village in Monguagon Township in 1923 and became a city in 1959. The Battle of Monguagon was fought here on August 9, 1812, and the Army ran a Nike missile launch site on what is now Young Patriot's Park in the 1950s and 1960s. The toll Wayne County Bridge connects Riverview to Grosse Ile.

None of that tells you whether your basement is dry, but it does tell you the city's housing is mostly mid-century and later, since the city was a village for decades and a city only from 1959. For a homeowner, that means a mix of older houses with original plumbing and newer ones. The structural drying page explains how a wet house of either kind is dried.

Questions people ask

Do you cover every ZIP code in Riverview?

Riverview has one delivery ZIP, 48193, which covers the city from Fort Street to West Jefferson Avenue and the Sibley Road side near I-75. Crews also work in nearby towns, so ask if you are just across the line.

Is emergency water removal available in Riverview?

Yes. The dispatch line answers 24 hours a day, every day, and a crew can be sent from Taylor to Riverview when one is available. Call and describe the water.

Is my Riverview home in a flood zone?

Look up your address on the FEMA Flood Map Service Center. The city building department can explain local rules.

Is river flood water cleaned differently?

Yes. Outside flood water is treated as contaminated, so porous materials are usually removed and surfaces are disinfected.

What if the water came from a failed pipe instead of a flood?

Clean supply water is handled differently. Describe the source to the dispatcher so the crew arrives with the right plan.
