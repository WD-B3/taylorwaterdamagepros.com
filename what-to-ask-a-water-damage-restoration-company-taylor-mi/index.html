What to Ask a Water Damage Restoration Company Before You Hire: Taylor, MI

The first call decides a lot. These questions help you tell a real answer from a sales pitch.

By the Taylor Water Damage Pros team

☎ (734) 865-2522

When water is on the floor, people hire the first company that answers. That is understandable, and it is also how people end up with surprise bills and unfinished jobs. A short list of questions takes five minutes and filters most of the problems.

Questions about the work

  1. Do your crews follow ANSI/IICRC S500, the published industry standard for water damage restoration?
  2. Will you give me a written scope of work before work begins, and does it list rooms, materials and equipment?
  3. How will you decide what can be dried and what must be removed?
  4. Will you take moisture readings each day and share them with me?
  5. What happens if the readings show the structure is not drying?

Note the first question. The S500 standard is a published reference, and a company can say whether its crews work to it. Be careful of vague answers such as we do it the right way. A real answer names the standard and how it is applied. The daily-readings question matters because it is how a crew knows when to stop. The method is explained on the structural drying page.

The Michigan licensing question

Michigan's Department of Licensing and Regulatory Affairs says contractors offering work that totals $600 or more in labor and materials must be licensed by the department. It advises checking the department's License Information database, or calling the Residential Builders section at 517-241-9309, before you hire.

Not all emergency water removal counts as the kind of work that license covers, and rebuilding work after drying often does. Ask the company which part of the job needs a license and who holds it. A good company will explain the difference without defensiveness.

Questions about money

  • Is the price in writing before work starts, with a breakdown for removal, drying and rebuild?
  • What is not included, such as permits or contents handling?
  • What are the payment terms? Michigan's licensing page advises not paying the full amount upfront.
  • Will you provide receipts and invoices I can give my insurer?
  • What happens if the scope changes after work starts?

The state licensing page also advises getting agreements in writing and checking a contractor's permanent address and phone, insurance and warranty. For emergency work, you may not have time to compare three estimates, so compare the answers to these questions instead.

Red flags on the first call

  • Pressure to sign something before anyone has seen the damage.
  • A price quoted over the phone for work nobody has scoped.
  • No physical address, or an address that does not match the company name.
  • A refusal to explain what the equipment is for.
  • Any promise about what your insurer will pay.

A real company is comfortable with questions. If the answers get shorter as the questions get more specific, call someone else. Emergency work leaves little time to shop around, but the minute spent on this list is still worth it.

Keep a record from the first minute

Write down the name of the person you spoke to, the time of the call and what was promised. Keep every written scope, invoice and receipt in one folder. If a dispute comes later, a clear paper trail settles most of it, and it also makes your insurance conversation simpler.

Questions about timing

Ask when a crew can be on site and what happens if they are delayed. Ask how long equipment is likely to stay and who checks the readings. We do not promise a fixed arrival time, and you should be wary of anyone who does without knowing your address and the traffic. A straight answer sounds like it has conditions.

Taylor, MI: permits and who to ask

Repairs after water damage can involve permits, depending on the work. Permit rules vary, so ask the City of Taylor building department which ones apply before a rebuild starts, and ask the contractor who will pull them. The state licensing page lists permits among the items a contract should include. A company that says permits are never needed is giving you an unreliable answer.

You can also verify a contractor's state license on the Licensing and Regulatory Affairs database, and ask for local references. Sources used: Michigan Department of Licensing and Regulatory Affairs guidance for homeowners and the ANSI/IICRC S500 published standard.

Questions people ask

Should I get three estimates after a water emergency?

For emergency drying, speed matters, so compare answers to the questions above instead. For the rebuild, get more than one estimate.

How do I check a Michigan contractor's license?

Use the Licensing and Regulatory Affairs License Information database, or call the Residential Builders section at 517-241-9309.

What should I ask about permits in Taylor?

Ask the City of Taylor building department which permits apply, and ask the contractor who will obtain them.

Is a company that promises insurance approval trustworthy?

No. Only your insurer decides coverage. A good company documents the loss and explains the scope.
