Does Homeowners Insurance Cover Water Damage? A Taylor, MI Guide

Sometimes yes and sometimes no. The cause of the water decides, and so do the words you use on the first call.

By the Taylor Water Damage Pros team

☎ (734) 865-2522

Most Taylor homeowners find out what their policy covers on the worst possible day. The short version is that homeowners policies tend to cover sudden and accidental water damage, tend to exclude gradual damage and neglect, and usually do not cover flooding. Your actual policy language decides, so use this guide as a map, not as a promise.

Sudden and accidental versus gradual

The line insurers draw is between a sudden event and a slow one. A supply line that bursts, a water heater tank that fails or an appliance hose that splits are sudden. A pipe that dripped for months behind a wall, a roof that leaked for several seasons or seepage from poor maintenance are gradual. Coverage for the first is common, and coverage for the second is often excluded.

Flood is its own category

Michigan's Department of Insurance and Financial Services states that flood damage is not covered under a standard homeowners policy. Separate flood cover comes from the National Flood Insurance Program or a private insurer. FEMA calls floods the most common and most costly natural disaster and says you do not have to be in a flood zone to experience one.

Sewer and water backup endorsements offer limited coverage, according to the same agency, so ask your agent exactly what yours says. The cleanup for that kind of event is covered on the sewage backup page, and rising outside water is covered on the flood damage page.

The words you use on the first call

DIFS gives specific advice on this. Contact your insurer's claims hotline or your agent with your policy number ready, tell them you have water damage and want to learn what coverage applies, and do not describe the event as a flood. A sewer backup or a failing sump pump may be covered under your policy, which is why the agency advises against labeling all water damage a flood.

  • Have your policy number and the date you discovered the damage ready.
  • Describe what you saw and what you did, without guessing at the cause.
  • Ask what is covered, what your deductible is and what documents are needed.
  • Take steps to protect the property from further damage, such as shutting off water.
  • Write down the name of the person you spoke to and the date and time.

Protect the property, and keep the proof

Policies expect you to limit further damage, and drying a house quickly is part of that. DIFS also advises taking photos or videos of damaged items and not discarding them until your insurer tells you to. A restoration company that logs moisture readings and writes a scope of work gives the adjuster something concrete to review, but the coverage decision belongs to the insurer.

If your loss started as a pipe failure, the plan on the burst pipe page covers what to shut off. For a loss that came through the roof, read the storm water damage page to see how it differs from flooding.

Deductibles, renters and the cost of small claims

A deductible is the part of the loss you pay. If the repair bill is close to it, a claim may not pay much. Renters have a different situation, since the building owner's policy covers the building and a renter's policy covers belongings. Ask both who pays for what before you start.

Taylor, MI: what the numbers say about your policy

Taylor had 63,409 residents at the 2020 Census, and 66.7 percent of homes were owner-occupied for 2020 to 2024. The median value of an owner-occupied home was $152,600 in that period. That matters because a repair bill measured against a deductible is a bigger share of a home that costs less, so Taylor owners are more likely than owners of expensive houses to be near the edge of whether a claim is worth filing.

The state regulator's page for flooding notes that a single inch of water could cost a homeowner $10,000 or more in repairs, using an illustration that applies to the country as a whole. Use it as a sense of scale, not a quote for your house.

Ask your agent about flood coverage, since you can look up whether your address is in a mapped flood zone on the FEMA Flood Map Service Center. Sources used: Michigan DIFS flood guidance and the U.S. Census Bureau QuickFacts.

Questions people ask

Does a Taylor, MI homeowner need flood insurance if the home is outside a mapped flood zone?

FEMA says a flood can happen outside a mapped zone, and a standard homeowners policy does not cover flood. Look up your address on the FEMA Flood Map Service Center and ask your agent whether separate flood coverage makes sense.

Should I say flood or water damage when I call my insurer?

Michigan's insurance regulator advises saying you have water damage and asking what coverage applies, because a sewer backup or sump failure may be covered while flood is not.

Is the water heater itself covered if it caused the damage?

Often the damage the heater caused is covered when it fails suddenly, while the heater itself is treated as a maintenance item. Ask your insurer how yours treats it.

What if my claim is denied?

Ask the insurer to explain the denial in writing, compare it with your policy and contact the state insurance regulator if you believe it is wrong.
