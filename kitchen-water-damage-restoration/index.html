Kitchen Water Damage Restoration in Taylor, MI

Sink bases, cabinets and the floor beneath them take the hit when a kitchen line fails. Dispatch is open 24/7.

☎ (734) 865-2522

Kitchen water damage restoration in Taylor, MI is about the cabinets, countertops, flooring and walls around a source that is usually small and constant: a sink supply, a drain, a dishwasher line or a refrigerator line.

The kitchen is crowded with materials that react badly to water, and the cabinet base is often the first to swell. If you see water, close the shutoff valves under the sink, empty the cabinet and call the dispatch line.

This page owns the kitchen as a room. The failed machine itself is on the appliance leak page, and a supply line that burst is on the burst pipe page. The questions here are about what survives, what has to come out and how the room dries.

What happens to cabinets

Cabinet construction decides the outcome. Particleboard and medium-density fiberboard swell when wet and do not return to their shape, so a wet particleboard sink base is usually replaced. Plywood boxes tolerate water better and can often be dried. Solid wood doors and face frames can often be saved if they are dried quickly. Laminate and thermofoil surfaces can bubble and peel when water gets behind them.

Under the sink and behind the toe kick

Water collects under cabinets where you cannot see it. The toe kick, the cabinet floor and the subfloor are the usual hiding places, and a leak under the sink can travel along the back wall into adjoining cabinets. The crew removes the toe kick, lifts or opens the base and reads moisture in the subfloor and wall before deciding what comes out.

A kitchen island with a sink, or a dishwasher beside a wall, makes the picture larger. Drying a wet kitchen is described on the structural drying page, and the wall behind it follows the wall and drywall page.

Floors and the room around the kitchen

Kitchen floors vary. Vinyl and tile can be dried in place while the subfloor beneath is checked. Hardwood, which is covered on the hardwood floor page, can cup or buckle, and laminate can swell at the seams. Because kitchens connect to dining rooms, the damage often runs into other floors, and the carpet page applies where carpet meets the kitchen.

  • Empty the wet cabinet and photograph everything before it is moved.
  • Do not run the dishwasher or sink until the source is fixed.
  • Keep food, small appliances and valuables out of the wet area.
  • Save a sample of the cabinet and countertop, so replacements can be matched.
  • Call a plumber for the source and call us for the drying.

Kitchen restoration cost factors

Price follows how many cabinets are affected, the cabinet materials, the countertop and flooring, the drying days and the rebuild. Custom cabinets cost more to match than stock ones, and a partial replacement often raises the question of matching the finish. Scope and price are explained before work starts.

For insurance, the state regulator's practical steps apply: contact your insurer or agent with your policy number, say you have water damage and take photos or video. Keep damaged cabinets and parts until the insurer says they can be discarded. Sudden and accidental damage is often covered, while a slow drip under a sink that went unrepaired often is not. Questions people ask

Can wet kitchen cabinets be saved?

Often, if they are plywood or solid wood and are dried quickly. Particleboard boxes that have swollen usually need to be replaced.

Do you handle kitchen water damage emergencies at night in Taylor?

Yes. The dispatch line is open 24 hours a day. A running supply line is urgent, and the dispatcher can tell you how to stop it, with same-day response when crews are available.

How do I know if the subfloor under the cabinets is wet?

A moisture meter reads it. Signs include a soft toe kick, a musty smell and swelling at the cabinet bottom.

Can the countertop stay?

Stone and solid surface usually can. Laminate over a swollen substrate usually cannot.

Will the new cabinets match?

Stock cabinets are easier to match than custom ones. Keep a door or drawer front as a sample so a close match can be found.
